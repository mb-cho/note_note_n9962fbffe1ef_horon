European Great Power Intervention and Encroachment on the Ottoman Empire

By 1876, confronting nationalist uprisings in the Balkans, predatory diplomatic and military pressures from the European Great Powers, and calamitous state bankruptcy, the Ottoman Empire stood on the brink of unprecedented ruin—its territories and sovereign prerogatives subjected to progressive dismemberment and "encroachment" (piecemeal erosion like silkworms consuming mulberry leaves).[1][2]

🔥 1. Encroachment from Within and Without: The Dual Siege of Finance and Territory

The existential crisis culminating in 1876 was driven by a twin siege: the financial and economic colonization orchestrated by Western merchant banking capital, proceeding hand-in-hand with territorial dismemberment led by the Russian Empire and rival European powers.

🏦 ① Sovereign Insolvency (Economic & Financial Encroachment)
Beginning with its first foreign loan during the Crimean War (1854), the Sublime Porte relied ever more heavily on British and French bond markets to finance modernizing reforms (Tanzimat), wartime mobilization, and palace luxuries.

Crushed by punishing coupon yields and substantial underwriting discounts, the Ottoman Treasury was unable to sustain interest service, formally declaring partial default in October 1875. This surrender effectively placed imperial fiscal and tax-collecting sovereignty under the direct tutelage of European creditor syndicates.[2][3]
⚔️ ② Balkan Insurrections & Great Power Intervention (Territorial Encroachment)
Following the 1875 peasant revolt in Bosnia and Herzegovina, violent rebellion flared across Bulgaria in 1876, swiftly followed by declarations of war from Serbia and Montenegro.

Seizing an opportune path toward the Mediterranean under the mantle of Pan-Slavism and the protection of Orthodox Christians, the Russian Empire mobilized for intervention, while the Great Powers convened international conferences under the aegis of the "Eastern Question" to partition Ottoman realms.[4][5]
HISTORICAL ARCHETYPE OF THE DEBT TRAP
【The Classic Archetype of Sovereign Dispossession by Financial Capital】
Following the 1875 default, the sultan was compelled in 1881 to issue the Decree of Muharram, establishing the Ottoman Public Debt Administration (OPDA). Representatives of British, French, German, and Austrian creditors took over Istanbul's tax machinery, directly intercepting revenues from salt, tobacco, spirits, silk tithes, and customs duties to service foreign bonds. This apparatus established the historical archetype of turning a sovereign state into a debt-peonage financial colony.

📜 2. Resistance to Encroachment: The Apex of the Tanzimat Modernization Reforms

To forestall the imminent disintegration and partition of the empire, constitutionalist reformers led by Grand Vizier Midhat Pasha launched a daring institutional counteroffensive through radical constitutional modernization.[2][6]

May–August 1876
👑 Palace Revolutions & Imperial Succession
Sultan Abdülaziz, held responsible for sovereign insolvency and obstruction of reform, was deposed in a palace coup. His successor, Murad V, was removed shortly thereafter due to psychological incapacity, bringing the reform-pledged Abdul Hamid II to the throne.[2][6]
December 23, 1876
⚖️ Proclamation of the Midhat Constitution (Asia's First Written Constitution)
Timed precisely to coincide with the opening session of the Constantinople Conference—where the Great Powers presented partition ultimatums—the Ottoman government promulgated the Ottoman Constitution (Midhat Constitution) to demonstrate that the empire was a modern constitutional state, eliminating the pretext for foreign intervention.

A representative parliament was established, declaring all imperial subjects—regardless of religion or ethnicity—equal citizens under the umbrella of "Ottomanism" (Osmanlılık) to prevent the secession of Balkan nationalities.[6][7][8][9][11][12]

🥀 3. The Denouement and Prelude to Autocracy: The Russo-Turkish War and Constitutional Suspension

💥 The Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)
Constitutional diplomacy failed to stop Russian ambitions. In April 1877, Tsar Alexander II declared war. Russian forces broke through Balkan fortifications, capturing Plevna and advancing to San Stefano, on the direct outskirts of Istanbul.[5]
🔒 Constitutional Suspension & Autocracy (1878)
Citing wartime emergency, Abdul Hamid II banished Midhat Pasha and, in February 1878, indefinitely suspended the constitution and dissolved parliament, instituting thirty years of autocratic rule.[10][13]
🗺️ The Treaty of Berlin & Dismemberment (1878)
At the Congress of Berlin (1878) mediated by Bismarck, Serbia, Montenegro, and Romania were granted full sovereignty, Bulgaria became an autonomous principality, Austria-Hungary occupied Bosnia, and Britain occupied Cyprus, solidifying the empire's dismemberment.[5]

🌐 5. Strategic Calculations and Encroachment Methods of the Great Powers in the "Eastern Question"

Great Power Geopolitical & Financial Objectives Concrete Actions & Encroachment on Ottoman Sovereignty
🇬🇧 Great Britain Securing imperial lifelines to British India (Suez Canal, Eastern Mediterranean) and enforcing London bondholder debt recovery. Restrained full Russian expansion while exacting territorial concessions: military occupation of Cyprus (1878) and Egypt (1882), taking control of Egyptian and Ottoman debt syndicates.
🇫🇷 France Protecting Paris bourses as the largest holder of Ottoman foreign bonds; expanding commercial dominance across North Africa and the Levant. Co-managed the Imperial Ottoman Bank and drove the creation of the OPDA. Occupied and established a protectorate over Ottoman Tunisia in 1881.
🇷🇺 Russian Empire Controlling the Turkish Straits (Bosporus and Dardanelles) and achieving direct naval breakout into the Mediterranean. Championed Pan-Slavism to arm Balkan rebels; launched the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, forcing the creation of Greater Bulgaria and extracting enormous war indemnities.
🇩🇪 German Empire
(Bismarck to Wilhelm II)		 Initially arbitrated as the "honest broker" between rivals; subsequently pivoted toward economic penetration via the Berlin-Baghdad Railway (3B Policy). Hosted the 1878 Congress of Berlin. In the 1890s, Deutsche Bank secured concessions for railway construction and mineral exploitation, directly challenging British-French financial supremacy.
