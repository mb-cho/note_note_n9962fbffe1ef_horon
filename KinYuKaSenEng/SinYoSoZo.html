Mechanics and Risks of Credit Creation

Credit creation is the mechanism through which the commercial banking system repeatedly relends deposits, thereby generating deposit currency (money) across society as a whole that is many times the initial deposit amount.[1][2]

💡 Core Concepts of Credit Creation

💳 Deposit Currency
Money that exists as digital ledger entries in bank accounts, directly usable for settlements such as bank wire transfers and electronic payments.[1]
🛡️ Reserve Requirement Ratio
The fraction of customer deposits that commercial banks are legally mandated to retain on reserve (e.g., at the central bank) to ensure withdrawal liquidity.[1][3]
💰 Primary Deposit
The initial cash deposited from outside into the commercial banking system, which serves as the foundational reserve base for credit expansion.[1][3][4][5][6]

📊 Step-by-Step Mechanism (Assuming a 10% Reserve Requirement)

1
Stage 1: Receipt of the Primary Deposit
Cash of ¥1,000,000 is deposited into Bank A.[1]
2
Stage 2: Initial Credit Extension
Bank A retains ¥100,000 (10% reserve requirement) in reserves and lends the remaining ¥900,000 to Person X.[1]
3
Stage 3: Redeposit Through Settlement
Person X spends the ¥900,000 in commercial transactions, and the recipient, Person Y, deposits the full ¥900,000 into Bank B.[1]
4
Stage 4: Secondary Lending Chain
Bank B retains ¥90,000 (10%) in reserves and lends ¥810,000 to another borrower (Person Z).[1]
★
Final Outcome: Multiplier Expansion of Deposit Currency
As the cascading cycle of "Deposit → Loan → Deposit" repeats across the banking system, theoretically a total of approximately ¥10,000,000 in deposit currency is created throughout society.[1][2]
Theoretical Calculation of Credit Creation (Money Multiplier)
Total Deposits = Primary Deposit ÷ Reserve Requirement Ratio

With a 10% reserve requirement: ¥1,000,000 ÷ 0.10 = ¥10,000,000
(Newly created derivative deposit currency: ¥10,000,000 − ¥1,000,000 = ¥9,000,000)

⚠️ Systemic Risks and Vulnerabilities

🏃💨 Bank Run (Panics)
A phenomenon where loss of confidence prompts depositors to rush to bank branches simultaneously to withdraw cash. Because vault cash and central bank reserves represent only a fraction of total deposits (e.g., 10%), banks immediately suffer liquidity shortfalls, threatening even solvent institutions with contagious collapse and systemic gridlock.[3]
💥 Insolvency & Loan Defaults
When borrowers default due to economic downturns or speculative collapse, non-performing loans severely impair bank assets. Hindered credit recovery impedes new credit extensions, abruptly halting the credit creation mechanism and triggering severe credit contraction (a sharp drop in money supply and a deflationary debt spiral).[3]
